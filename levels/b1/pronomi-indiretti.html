Pronomi Indiretti

Mi, ti, gli, le, ci, vi, gli — replacing "to/for someone".

ObjectivesExplanationRulesExamplesCommon MistakesPracticeSummaryRelated

Introduction

An indirect object answers "to whom?" or "for whom?" — in English it's often marked by "to", but in Italian, once replaced by a pronoun, that "to" disappears entirely.

By the end of this lesson you can…

  • Identify an indirect object and choose the matching pronoun
  • Distinguish gli (to him) from le (to her)
  • Use common verbs that take an indirect object: dare, dire, scrivere, telefonare, piacere

Explanation

Explanation

Many indirect object pronouns look identical to the direct ones you already know (mi, ti, ci, vi) — only the third person differs: gli (to him), le (to her, also the formal "to you"), and gli again for the plural "to them" (loro is the old, formal alternative, placed after the verb, but gli before the verb is now standard in speech).

Grammar Rules

The Rules

a) Verbs that take an indirect object

dare (to give), dire (to say/tell), scrivere (to write), telefonare (to phone), chiedere (to ask), rispondere (to answer) — all describe an action directed "to/for" someone.

b) Piacere — a special case

Piacere (to like/please) works backwards from English: the thing liked is the subject, and the person is an indirect object. Mi piace il caffè. (Coffee is pleasing to me = I like coffee.) Mi piacciono i film. (plural subject → plural verb piacciono)

Examples

See It in Action

  • Gli scrivo un'email ogni settimana.
    I write him an email every week.
  • Le ho telefonato ieri.
    I called her yesterday.
  • Ci hanno detto la verità.
    They told us the truth.
  • Mi piace molto questa città.
    I really like this city.
  • Non gli piacciono i dolci.
    He doesn't like sweets.

Common Mistakes

Watch Out For

AvoidGli scrivo a Maria. (using gli for a woman)

Use insteadLe scrivo.

Gli is masculine ("to him"); a female indirect object needs le.

AvoidMi piaccio il gelato.

Use insteadMi piace il gelato.

Piacere agrees with the thing liked (il gelato, singular), not with the speaker — mi piaccio would mean "I like myself".

AvoidTelefono a lei ogni giorno. (natural but the pronoun form is preferred in fluent speech)

Use insteadLe telefono ogni giorno.

While a lei is grammatical, fluent Italian strongly prefers the unstressed pronoun le before the verb in everyday speech.

Summary

Review

  • Indirect object pronouns: mi, ti, gli (to him), le (to her/formal you), ci, vi, gli (to them).
  • Verbs like dare, dire, scrivere, telefonare, chiedere naturally take an indirect object.
  • Piacere works backwards from English: the thing liked is the subject, agreeing with the verb.
