I Superlativi

The most/least..., and the intensifier -issimo.

ObjectivesExplanationRulesExamplesCommon MistakesPracticeSummaryRelated

Introduction

Italian has two different kinds of superlative: the relative ("the most... of the group") and the absolute ("extremely...", with no comparison at all) — and the second is one of the most distinctive features of everyday Italian.

By the end of this lesson you can…

  • Form the relative superlative (the most/least ... of/in)
  • Form the absolute superlative with -issimo
  • Recognize irregular comparative/superlative forms: migliore, peggiore, meglio, peggio

Explanation

Explanation

The relative superlative reuses the comparative più/meno, adding the definite article: il più alto della classe (the tallest in the class). The absolute superlative replaces the adjective's final vowel with -issimo/a/i/e, meaning "extremely/very" with no comparison implied at all: bellissimo (extremely beautiful) is stronger and more common in speech than molto bello.

Grammar Rules

The Rules

a) Relative superlative

Article + (noun +) più/meno + adjective + di: È la ragazza più intelligente della classe. (She's the smartest girl in the class.)

b) Absolute superlative — -issimo

Drop the final vowel, add -issimo/a/i/e: alto → altissimo, facile → facilissimo, buona → buonissima. Words ending in -co/-go often add an h to keep the hard sound: stanco → stanchissimo.

c) Irregular comparatives/superlatives

  • buono → migliore (better) → il migliore/ottimo (the best/excellent)
  • cattivo → peggiore (worse) → il peggiore/pessimo (the worst/terrible)
  • As adverbs: bene → meglio (better), male → peggio (worse)

Examples

See It in Action

  • Questo è il ristorante migliore della città.
    This is the best restaurant in the city.
  • Sono stanchissima oggi.
    I'm extremely tired today.
  • Il Colosseo è il monumento più famoso di Roma.
    The Colosseum is the most famous monument in Rome.
  • Questa torta è buonissima!
    This cake is delicious!
  • Oggi mi sento meglio di ieri.
    Today I feel better than yesterday.

Common Mistakes

Watch Out For

Avoidmolto bellissimo

Use insteadbellissimo (already means "very beautiful")

-issimo already means "extremely" — adding molto in front is redundant, doubling the intensifier.

Avoidpiù buono for the irregular comparative

Use insteadmigliore

Buono has an irregular comparative, migliore — più buono is understood but non-standard where migliore is expected.

AvoidSto bene meglio.

Use insteadSto meglio.

Meglio already is the comparative of bene ("better") — bene meglio combines two forms of the same idea.

Summary

Review

  • Relative superlative: article + più/meno + adjective + di (the most/least ... in/of).
  • Absolute superlative: -issimo/a/i/e, meaning "extremely", with no molto needed.
  • Buono/cattivo/bene/male have irregular comparatives and superlatives: migliore, peggiore, meglio, peggio.
