Ci e Ne

Two small particles that replace a place, a topic, or a quantity.

ObjectivesExplanationRulesExamplesCommon MistakesPracticeSummaryRelated

Introduction

You already met ci inside c'è/ci sono — now meet its full range, alongside its partner particle ne, both of which let you avoid repeating a place, topic, or quantity you've already mentioned.

By the end of this lesson you can…

  • Use ci to replace a place or a topic introduced by a, in, su
  • Use ne to replace a quantity or a topic introduced by di
  • Place ci and ne correctly relative to the verb

Explanation

Explanation

Ci replaces a place ("there") or a phrase introduced by a/in/su: Vai a Roma? Sì, ci vado domani. (Are you going to Rome? Yes, I'm going [there] tomorrow.) Ne replaces a quantity, or a phrase introduced by di: Quanti libri hai? Ne ho tre. (How many books do you have? I have three [of them].)

Grammar Rules

The Rules

a) Ci — place and topic (a/in/su)

  • Place: Sei mai stato in Sicilia? Sì, ci sono stato l'anno scorso.
  • Topic (pensare a): Pensi al tuo futuro? Sì, ci penso spesso.

b) Ne — quantity and topic (di)

  • Quantity: Vuoi del vino? Sì, ne vorrei un po'.
  • Topic (parlare di): Parli di politica? Non ne parlo mai.

c) Position

Both go before a conjugated verb, or attach to an infinitive: Ci vado. / Voglio andarci. With a number/quantity + passato prossimo, the participle agrees with ne's quantity: Quante mele hai comprato? Ne ho comprate cinque.

Examples

See It in Action

  • Vai spesso in palestra? Sì, ci vado tre volte a settimana.
    Do you go to the gym often? Yes, I go [there] three times a week.
  • Quanti fratelli hai? Ne ho due.
    How many siblings do you have? I have two [of them].
  • Credi in questo progetto? Sì, ci credo davvero.
    Do you believe in this project? Yes, I really believe in it.
  • Vuoi ancora del caffè? No grazie, non ne voglio più.
    Do you want more coffee? No thanks, I don't want any more [of it].
  • Ne ho parlato con il mio capo.
    I talked about it with my boss.

Common Mistakes

Watch Out For

AvoidVado ci domani.

Use insteadCi vado domani.

Ci goes before the conjugated verb, exactly like other object pronouns — not after it.

AvoidHo comprato cinque.

Use insteadNe ho comprate cinque.

A bare number needs ne to stand in for "of them" — Italian can't drop the quantity's referent the way casual English sometimes does.

AvoidNe ho comprato cinque mele. (both ne and the noun)

Use insteadHo comprato cinque mele. / Ne ho comprate cinque.

Ne replaces the noun — using both ne and the noun together in the same clause is redundant.
