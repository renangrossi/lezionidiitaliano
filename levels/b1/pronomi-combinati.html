Pronomi Combinati

Me lo, te lo, glielo — direct and indirect pronouns fused into one word.

ObiettiviSpiegazioneRegoleEsempiErrori ComuniPraticaRiepilogoContinua

Introduzione

When both a direct and an indirect object pronoun appear in the same sentence — "I'll give it to you" — Italian fuses them into a single combined form, always indirect before direct.

Al termine di questa lezione saprai…

  • Combine indirect and direct object pronouns in the correct order
  • Recognize the mi/ti/ci/vi → me/te/ce/ve vowel change
  • Recognize glielo/gliela/glieli/gliele as one fused word

Spiegazione

Spiegazione

Mi, ti, ci, vi change their final -i to -e before a direct object pronoun (mi + lo → me lo). Gli and le both become glie- and physically fuse with the direct pronoun into one word: gli/le + lo → glielo, gli/le + la → gliela, and so on — this single form covers "to him/her/them" plus the direct object, disambiguated only by context.

Regole Grammaticali

Le Regole

a) Order and position

Indirect always comes first, direct second, and both go before the conjugated verb (or attach to an infinitive): Te lo do. (I give it to you.) Posso dartelo. / Te lo posso dare.

b) Participle agreement

The past participle agrees with the direct object exactly as with lo/la/li/le alone: Te l'ho dato. (m. sing.) Te l'ho data. (f. sing., elided the same way)

Esempi

Vediamola in Pratica

  • Il libro? Te lo do domani.
    The book? I'll give it to you tomorrow.
  • Le chiavi? Gliele ho lasciate sul tavolo.
    The keys? I left them for him/her on the table.
  • Ce lo ha spiegato bene.
    He/she explained it to us well.
  • Non ve lo posso dire.
    I can't tell it to you [pl.].
  • Se vuoi il mio numero, te lo scrivo.
    If you want my number, I'll write it down for you.

Errori Comuni

Attenzione a

EvitaLo mi do.

Usa inveceMe lo do.

The indirect pronoun always comes before the direct one, and both come before the verb — never after in this order.

EvitaGli lo do.

Usa inveceGlielo do.

Gli/le physically fuse with lo/la/li/le into one written word — they never stay separate.

EvitaTe l'ho dato. (for una lettera, feminine)

Usa inveceTe l'ho data.

The participle must agree with the feminine direct object (la lettera), even though it's now hidden inside the elided l'.

Ripasso
