L'Imperativo Formale

Commands and instructions with Lei — and where pronouns go this time.

ObiettiviSpiegazioneRegoleEsempiErrori ComuniPraticaRiepilogoContinua

Introduzione

The formal imperative uses the Lei form — borrowed, interestingly, from the present subjunctive rather than the present indicative — and its pronoun placement rule is the opposite of the informal imperative's.

Al termine di questa lezione saprai…

  • Form the formal (Lei) imperative
  • Place pronouns correctly before the formal imperative
  • Contrast formal and informal imperative in the same situation

Spiegazione

Spiegazione

Where the informal imperative attaches pronouns to the end of the verb, the formal imperative places pronouns before the verb, exactly like any other conjugated verb form. The Lei imperative form itself looks like an -are verb's tu present tense and vice versa — a genuinely irregular-feeling swap worth memorizing as a pattern.

Regole Grammaticali

Le Regole

a) Formation

-are verbs take -i (the opposite of their tu present -i); -ere/-ire verbs take -a. Negation is simple: just add non before the form — no infinitive switch, unlike the informal tu.

b) Pronoun placement — before the verb

Mi dica! (Tell me! — formal) Si accomodi. (Please have a seat/come in.) Lo prenda pure. (Please take it.)

Imperativo formale (Lei) — parlare, prendere, aprire

Esempi

Vediamola in Pratica

  • Mi scusi, dov'è la stazione?
    Excuse me, where's the station? (Lei form of scusare)
  • Si accomodi, prego.
    Please, have a seat / come in.
  • Aspetti un momento, per favore.
    Please wait a moment.
  • Non si preoccupi.
    Don't worry. (formal)
  • Mi dica pure, come posso aiutarla?
    Go ahead and tell me, how can I help you?

Errori Comuni

Attenzione a

EvitaScusami! (to a stranger you'd address as Lei)

Usa inveceMi scusi!

Scusami is the informal tu imperative; a stranger or formal context needs the Lei form, mi scusi.

EvitaDicami!

Usa inveceMi dica!

Unlike the informal imperative, pronouns go before the formal (Lei) imperative verb, not attached after it.

EvitaNon aspettare! (as a formal negative command)

Usa inveceNon aspetti!

Only the informal tu negative imperative switches to the infinitive — the formal negative simply adds non before the normal Lei form.

Riepilogo

Ripasso

  • Formal (Lei) imperative: -are verbs take -i, -ere/-ire verbs take -a — the reverse of their present tense pattern.
  • Pronouns go before the formal imperative verb, unlike the informal, which attaches them after.
  • The formal negative is just non + the normal Lei form — no infinitive switch as with informal tu.
