Presente Indicativo dei Verbi Regolari

The present tense of -are, -ere and -ire verbs — Italian's three regular conjugations.

ObjectivesExplanationRulesExamplesCommon MistakesPracticeSummaryRelated

Introduction

Every Italian verb belongs to one of three families, named for their infinitive ending: -are, -ere, -ire. Learn the pattern for one regular verb in each family, and you can conjugate hundreds of others the same way.

By the end of this lesson you can…

  • Conjugate regular -are, -ere and -ire verbs in the present tense
  • Recognize -isc- verbs, a common pattern within -ire
  • Use the present tense for habits, facts and (unlike English) near-future plans

Explanation

Explanation

To conjugate a regular verb, drop the infinitive ending (-are/-ere/-ire) and add the ending for each subject. A subgroup of -ire verbs (like capire, to understand) inserts -isc- before the ending in all forms except noi/voi — there's no way to predict which -ire verbs do this, so it's learned per verb.

The present tense also covers ground English splits into two tenses: Parlo italiano can mean both “I speak Italian” and, right now, “I am speaking Italian”. It's also commonly used for near-future plans: Domani parto per Roma (Tomorrow I'm leaving for Rome).

Grammar Rules

The Rules

a) -are verbs (e.g. parlare, lavorare, abitare)

Endings: -o, -i, -a, -iamo, -ate, -ano

b) -ere verbs (e.g. scrivere, leggere, prendere)

Endings: -o, -i, -e, -iamo, -ete, -ono

c) -ire verbs (e.g. dormire, partire, aprire)

Endings: -o, -i, -e, -iamo, -ite, -ono

d) -isc- verbs (e.g. capire, finire, preferire, pulire)

Same -ire endings, but -isc- is inserted before the ending for io/tu/lui-lei/loro: capisco, capisci, capisce, capiamo, capite, capiscono.

Examples

See It in Action

  • Io lavoro in un ufficio.
    I work in an office.
  • Tu scrivi molte email.
    You write a lot of emails.
  • Lei dorme otto ore ogni notte.
    She sleeps eight hours every night.
  • Non capisco questa parola.
    I don't understand this word.
  • Domani partiamo per le vacanze.
    Tomorrow we're leaving for vacation.
  • Preferisco il tè al caffè.
    I prefer tea to coffee.

Common Mistakes

Watch Out For

AvoidIo parlo l'italiano fluentemente da domani.

Use insteadDa domani parlerò... / Parlo italiano ora, ma da domani...

Present tense for future plans works for near, planned events ("tomorrow I leave"), not for describing a change that will only become true later — that needs the future tense (covered at B1).

AvoidIo capo la lezione.

Use insteadIo capisco la lezione.

Capire is an -isc- verb: the io form is capisco, not a bare capo.

AvoidNoi parla italiano.

Use insteadNoi parliamo italiano.

Noi always takes the -iamo ending, never the lui/lei -a/-e ending.

Summary

Review

  • Three regular conjugations: -are (-o,-i,-a,-iamo,-ate,-ano), -ere (-o,-i,-e,-iamo,-ete,-ono), -ire (-o,-i,-e,-iamo,-ite,-ono).
  • Some -ire verbs insert -isc- before the ending, except for noi/voi.
  • The present tense covers habits, facts, right-now actions, and near-future plans.
