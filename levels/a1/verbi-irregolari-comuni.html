I Primi Verbi Irregolari: Andare, Fare, Volere

Three high-frequency irregular verbs to get you talking about going, doing, and wanting.

ObjectivesExplanationRulesExamplesCommon MistakesPracticeSummaryRelated

Introduction

Not every irregular verb waits until A2 — andare (to go), fare (to do/make) and volere (to want) are so common in everyday speech that you need them from the start.

By the end of this lesson you can…

  • Conjugate andare, fare and volere in the present tense
  • Use andare + a + infinitive for near-future plans
  • Use volere + infinitive to express wants

Explanation

Explanation

These three don't follow the regular -are/-ire patterns you just learned, so their forms simply need memorizing. The reward is immediate: andare + a + infinitive lets you talk about near-future plans (Vado a mangiare, I'm going to eat), and volere + infinitive is the simplest way to express a wish (Voglio partire, I want to leave).

Grammar Rules

The Rules

a) Andare + a + infinitive

Expresses "going to [do something]": Vado a studiare. (I'm going to study.) The a is required even though English doesn't need an equivalent word here.

b) Fare in fixed expressions

Fare is used for weather (Fa caldo, It's hot) and many activities that don't literally involve "making" anything: fare la spesa (to go grocery shopping), fare colazione (to have breakfast), fare una domanda (to ask a question).

c) Volere + infinitive

Directly followed by an infinitive, no preposition needed: Voglio imparare l'italiano. (I want to learn Italian.)

Examples

See It in Action

  • Vado a lavorare.
    I'm going to work.
  • Cosa fai stasera?
    What are you doing tonight?
  • Voglio un caffè, grazie.
    I want a coffee, thanks.
  • Faccio colazione alle otto.
    I have breakfast at eight.
  • Vogliamo andare al mare.
    We want to go to the sea.
  • Loro vanno in vacanza domani.
    They're going on vacation tomorrow.

Common Mistakes

Watch Out For

AvoidVado studiare.

Use insteadVado a studiare.

Andare requires the preposition a before an infinitive — it can never be dropped.

AvoidIo voglio a mangiare.

Use insteadIo voglio mangiare.

Volere is followed directly by the infinitive, with no preposition — unlike andare.

AvoidNoi vado al cinema.

Use insteadNoi andiamo al cinema.

Noi always needs its own form, andiamo, not the io form vado.

Summary

Review

  • Andare, fare and volere are irregular and must be memorized: vado/vai/va/andiamo/andate/vanno, etc.
  • Andare + a + infinitive expresses near-future plans; the a is mandatory.
  • Volere + infinitive expresses a want, with no preposition needed.
