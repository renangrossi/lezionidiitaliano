I Verbi Modali: Dovere, Potere, Volere

Must, can, and want — the three modal verbs that pair with an infinitive.

ObiettiviSpiegazioneRegoleEsempiErrori ComuniPraticaRiepilogoContinua

Introduzione

Dovere, potere and volere are Italian's three core modal verbs — each pairs directly with an infinitive to add a layer of meaning: obligation, ability/permission, or desire.

Al termine di questa lezione saprai…

  • Conjugate dovere, potere and volere in the present tense
  • Distinguish obligation (dovere), ability/permission (potere), and desire (volere)
  • Use modal verbs correctly with a following infinitive

Spiegazione

Spiegazione

All three are irregular, and all three are followed directly by a bare infinitive with no preposition in between — a simpler pattern than English's mix of "must", "have to", "can", "is able to". With reflexive verbs and object pronouns (covered later), the pronoun can attach either before the modal or to the end of the infinitive: Mi devo alzare / Devo alzarmi — both correct.

Regole Grammaticali

Le Regole

a) Dovere — obligation/necessity

Devo studiare stasera. (I have to study tonight.) Also used for probability: Deve essere tardi. (It must be late.)

b) Potere — ability/permission

Posso aprire la finestra? (Can/May I open the window? — both ability and permission use potere, unlike English's can/may distinction.)

c) Volere — desire

Voglio andare in vacanza. (I want to go on vacation.) A softer, more polite form is the conditional vorrei (I would like), introduced at B1.

Esempi

Vediamola in Pratica

  • Devo lavorare fino a tardi oggi.
    I have to work late today.
  • Puoi aiutarmi, per favore?
    Can you help me, please?
  • Non possiamo uscire, piove troppo.
    We can't go out, it's raining too much.
  • Vogliono comprare una casa nuova.
    They want to buy a new house.
  • Dovete finire il compito entro venerdì.
    You [pl.] have to finish the assignment by Friday.

Errori Comuni

Attenzione a

EvitaDevo di studiare.

Usa inveceDevo studiare.

Dovere is followed directly by the infinitive, with no preposition.

EvitaIo posso a nuotare bene.

Usa inveceIo posso nuotare bene.

Potere never takes a preposition before the infinitive.

EvitaLei deve essere tarda.

Usa inveceDeve essere tardi.

Tardi is an invariable adverb (late) here, not the adjective tarda — no agreement applies.

Riepilogo

Ripasso

  • Dovere = obligation/probability; potere = ability/permission; volere = desire.
  • All three are irregular and pair directly with a bare infinitive, no preposition.
  • Potere covers both English "can" and "may" — Italian doesn't distinguish them.
