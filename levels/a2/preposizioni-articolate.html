Preposizioni Articolate

When a, di, da, in and su fuse with the definite article.

ObjectivesExplanationRulesExamplesCommon MistakesPracticeSummaryRelated

Introduction

Five of Italian's simple prepositions fuse with a following definite article into a single word — a pattern you'll use constantly once you start referring to specific, known things.

By the end of this lesson you can…

  • Combine a, di, da, in and su with the definite article
  • Recall the fused forms al, del, dal, nel, sul and their variants
  • Choose the correct fused form based on the noun's article

Explanation

Explanation

Con and per also historically fuse (col, pel) but this is rare and old-fashioned in modern Italian — stick to con il/con i etc. The five that matter are a, di, da, in, su. Each combines with il/lo/la/l'/i/gli/le the same way, replacing the article's initial vowel or consonant pattern.

Grammar Rules

The Rules

a) When to fuse

Fuse the preposition whenever the noun that follows would itself take a definite article: Vado al cinema. (al = a + il, because "il cinema" needs il). No article, no fusion: Vado a scuola. (school with no article, a fixed expression).

b) Di as possession vs. dei/delle as "some"

Del/dello/della/dei/degli/delle can also mean "some" (the partitive article, full detail at B1): Vorrei del pane. (I'd like some bread.)

Examples

See It in Action

  • Il libro è sul tavolo.
    The book is on the table.
  • Andiamo al mare questo weekend.
    We're going to the seaside this weekend.
  • La chiave è nella borsa.
    The key is in the bag.
  • Parlo spesso degli amici che vivono all'estero.
    I often talk about the friends who live abroad.
  • Il treno arriva dalla stazione centrale.
    The train arrives from the central station.

Common Mistakes

Watch Out For

AvoidVado a il cinema.

Use insteadVado al cinema.

A + il must fuse into al — they're never written as two separate words.

AvoidIl libro è su il tavolo.

Use insteadIl libro è sul tavolo.

Su + il fuses into sul.

AvoidParlo delle amico.

Use insteadParlo dell'amico.

Amico is masculine singular, so the fused form must be dell' (di + l'), not the feminine plural delle.

Summary

Review

  • A, di, da, in and su fuse with the definite article whenever the noun needs one.
  • The pattern is regular across all five prepositions: al/allo/alla/all'/ai/agli/alle, and so on.
  • No article needed, no fusion — fixed expressions like a scuola, a casa stay unfused.
