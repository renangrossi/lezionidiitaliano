Aggettivi e Pronomi Possessivi

My, your, his/her — possessives that agree with the thing owned, not the owner.

ObiettiviSpiegazioneRegoleEsempiErrori ComuniPraticaRiepilogoContinua

Introduzione

Italian possessives are one of the biggest grammatical differences from English: they agree with the thing owned, not with the owner — so suo can mean "his" or "her", depending only on what's possessed and by whom in context.

Al termine di questa lezione saprai…

  • Use possessive adjectives that agree with the noun possessed
  • Use the article with most possessives, except for singular family members
  • Use possessive pronouns to avoid repeating the noun

Spiegazione

Spiegazione

Possessives are almost always preceded by the definite article, agreeing with both the article and the noun: il mio libro, la mia casa, i miei libri, le mie case. The one major exception is singular, unmodified family members, which drop the article: mio padre, tua sorella — but the article returns with a plural family noun or a modified one: i miei genitori, la mia sorella maggiore.

Regole Grammaticali

Le Regole

a) Agreement

Mio/tuo/suo/nostro/vostro each have four forms (-o/-a/-i/-e) agreeing with the noun possessed. Loro never changes: il loro libro, la loro casa, i loro libri.

b) Singular family members drop the article

  • mio fratello, tua madre, suo zio (no article)
  • but: i miei fratelli (plural — article returns)
  • and: la mia sorella maggiore (modified by an adjective — article returns)

c) Possessive pronouns

Same forms, used alone to avoid repeating the noun: Questo è il mio libro. Dov'è il tuo? (This is my book. Where's yours?)

Esempi

Vediamola in Pratica

  • Questa è la mia macchina.
    This is my car.
  • Mio fratello vive a Torino.
    My brother lives in Turin.
  • I loro figli studiano all'estero.
    Their children study abroad.
  • Il tuo è più grande del mio.
    Yours is bigger than mine.
  • Suo padre è medico.
    His/her father is a doctor.

Errori Comuni

Attenzione a

Evitail mio fratello

Usa invecemio fratello

Singular, unmodified family members drop the article — the article only returns for plurals or modified nouns.

Evitala sua libro

Usa inveceil suo libro

The possessive and article must agree with libro (masculine), not with the owner's gender.

Evitai loro figlio

Usa inveceil loro figlio / i loro figli

The article must match the number of the noun; loro itself never changes but the article and noun must agree with each other.

Riepilogo

Ripasso

  • Possessive adjectives agree with the thing possessed, not the owner — suo can mean his or her.
  • Almost always used with the article, except before a singular, unmodified family member.
  • Loro never changes form, but the article before it still agrees with the noun.
