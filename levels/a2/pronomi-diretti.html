Pronomi Diretti

Mi, ti, lo, la, ci, vi, li, le — replacing a direct object to avoid repeating it.

ObiettiviSpiegazioneRegoleEsempiErrori ComuniPraticaRiepilogoContinua

Introduzione

Once you've mentioned something, Italian — like English — lets you replace it with a pronoun instead of repeating the noun. Direct object pronouns go before the verb, which takes some getting used to for English speakers.

Al termine di questa lezione saprai…

  • Identify a direct object and choose the matching pronoun
  • Place direct object pronouns correctly before a conjugated verb
  • Make the past participle agree with lo/la/li/le in the passato prossimo

Spiegazione

Spiegazione

A direct object answers "what?" or "whom?" with no preposition: in Leggo il libro, il libro is the direct object, replaceable by lo: Lo leggo. Unlike English ("I read it"), the pronoun goes before the conjugated verb, not after.

Regole Grammaticali

Le Regole

a) Position

Before a conjugated verb: La conosco bene. Attached to an infinitive (dropping its final -e): Voglio conoscerla. (also correct: La voglio conoscere.)

b) Agreement in the passato prossimo

With avere, lo/la/li/le force the past participle to agree, even though avere verbs otherwise never agree: Ho comprato la macchina. → L'ho comprata. (Lo/la elide to l' before a vowel.)

Esempi

Vediamola in Pratica

  • Compro il pane. Lo compro ogni giorno.
    I buy bread. I buy it every day.
  • Vedi Maria stasera? Sì, la vedo alle otto.
    Are you seeing Maria tonight? Yes, I'm seeing her at eight.
  • Hai visto i miei occhiali? Non li trovo.
    Have you seen my glasses? I can't find them.
  • Ho comprato le mele e le ho lavate.
    I bought the apples and washed them.
  • Ti chiamo dopo, va bene?
    I'll call you later, okay?

Errori Comuni

Attenzione a

EvitaLeggo lo.

Usa inveceLo leggo.

The direct object pronoun goes before the conjugated verb, not after, unlike English.

EvitaHo comprato la e ho mangiato la. (for la mela)

Usa inveceL'ho comprata e l'ho mangiata.

Lo/la elide to l' before a vowel (ho), and the participle must agree: comprata, not comprato.

EvitaHo visto li ieri.

Usa inveceLi ho visti ieri.

The pronoun must precede the verb, and the participle agrees with li: visti.

Riepilogo

Ripasso

  • Direct object pronouns (mi, ti, lo, la, ci, vi, li, le) replace a direct object and go before the conjugated verb.
  • They can attach to an infinitive instead, dropping its final -e.
  • With avere in the passato prossimo, lo/la/li/le force participle agreement — the one exception to "avere never agrees".
